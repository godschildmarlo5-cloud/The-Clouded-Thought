God's Property

The Devil opened a pawn shop somewhere between fear and desire. A crooked little building hidden behind fame, behind addiction, behind power, behind every door marked: "There has to be more." The line stretched for miles. Politicians. Preachers. Artists. Kings of empty kingdoms. Broken people carrying broken dreams. Each one stepped forward holding something in trembling hands. "My soul," they said. The Devil smiled. He always smiles. It's bad for business when customers read the contract. So he took their offerings. Stamped them. Filed them. Laughed. And the whole time I stood in the corner of my own mind watching. Observing. The way I always do. Fragments of myself sat beside me. Fear lit a cigarette. Pride adjusted his crown. Addiction bounced his leg impatiently. Shame kept staring at the floor. "Maybe they're right," Fear whispered. "Maybe souls can be sold." The room went quiet. Then Understanding, an old man with galaxies in his eyes, began to laugh. Not a cruel laugh. A knowing laugh. The kind of laugh a father gives when a child thinks they can buy the moon. "Sell what?" he asked. Everyone turned. "Your soul." Understanding laughed harder. "You can't sell what was never yours." Silence. The Devil's smile twitched. Understanding stood. His bones sounded like ancient church doors opening. "You didn't create your soul." "You didn't breathe life into yourself." "You didn't stitch eternity into your spirit." "So how can you sell it?" Fear dropped his cigarette. Pride removed his crown. Even Addiction stopped shaking. Meanwhile, the Devil began gathering papers. Nervously. Quickly. Like a businessman being audited by Heaven. "You can surrender." "You can rebel." "You can wander." "You can wound yourself." "You can forget who you belong to." "But ownership?" The old man shook his head. "No." Then the walls of my mind cracked open. And I saw it. A courtroom larger than creation. Stars hanging like chandeliers. Angels standing as witnesses. Time itself sitting silently in the gallery. And there, above every throne, above every kingdom, above every accusation— God. Not speaking. Not needing to. Because His signature was already written across every human soul. The Devil looked small. Tiny. Like a thief trying to claim ownership of stolen sunlight. "You don't own them," the heavens seemed to say. "You never did." The Devil screamed. "But they chose me." The stars answered: "They chose darkness." "That isn't the same as ownership." Then I understood. The greatest lie wasn't that people sell their souls. The greatest lie was believing God stopped wanting them afterward. No. The Father still stands at the edge of every wilderness. Still calls names through addictions. Through pride. Through mistakes. Through graveyards of regret. Still says: "Come home." The fragments inside me began to disappear. Fear left first. Then Shame. Then Pride. Then all the voices that had spent years telling me I belonged to my failures. And in the silence that remained, I heard only one voice. Ancient. Gentle. Unshaken. "You are Mine." Not because I earned it. Not because I deserved it. Not because I never wandered. But because no created thing can outbid its Creator. And no thief, no darkness, no devil, can purchase what Heaven has already claimed. The pawn shop closed. The contracts burned. The lie collapsed. And somewhere beyond the noise of the world, God was still holding what was always His.
